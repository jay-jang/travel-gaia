> A Management Fee is the fee paid by a hotel owner to a hotel management company (the operator) for providing the day-to-day operational management of the property — staffing, sales, procurement, accounting, and brand-standards compliance — under a Hotel Management Agreement (HMA). It is typically structured as a Base Management Fee (a percentage of total hotel revenue, commonly 2–4 %) plus an Incentive Management Fee (a share of profit above a defined threshold), and is distinct from the Franchise Fee paid to a brand.

The Hotel Management Agreement (HMA) is the contract through which the owner and operator define the management fee structure. The Base Management Fee accrues monthly as a percentage of total hotel revenues (rooms, food and beverage, spa, parking, and all other revenue streams) and is treated as a fixed operating cost in the hotel's Profit & Loss statement before reaching GOP (Gross Operating Profit). USALI (Uniform System of Accounts for the Lodging Industry) classifies the base management fee as an undistributed operating expense appearing below the departmental income lines but above GOP, ensuring consistent reporting across properties. The Incentive Management Fee (IMF) is structured to align operator interests with owner profitability: it is typically paid from a pool defined by Owner's Priority Return (the minimum return to the owner before the operator shares in upside), Adjusted Gross Operating Profit (AGOP), or Net Operating Income (NOI) exceeding a hurdle rate. Global hotel management companies such as Marriott International, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG, and Accor generate a significant portion of their fee revenue from base and incentive management fees across managed properties. For properties where the brand and operator are the same company (an "integrated" hotel), a single management contract may bundle royalty and management services; for "asset-light" franchised hotels managed by independent operators, the two fees flow to separate parties.

# Distinctions
- **Management Fee** vs [Franchise Fee](/lodging/hotel-rate/franchise-fee.md) — A Management Fee is paid by the hotel owner to the hotel management company (operator) for the day-to-day operation of the property; a Franchise Fee is paid by the hotel owner to the brand company (franchisor) for the right to use the brand name, reservation system, and loyalty programme. The two fees are separate obligations to separate parties: management fee to the operator, franchise fee to the brand.
- **Management Fee** vs [GOPPAR](/lodging/hotel-rate/goppar.md) — GOPPAR (Gross Operating Profit per Available Room) is a hotel performance metric measuring GOP divided by available rooms; the Management Fee is a line-item operating cost that is deducted before GOP is calculated. A higher management fee directly reduces GOPPAR, making the fee structure one of the key variables tracked in hotel investment analysis.
