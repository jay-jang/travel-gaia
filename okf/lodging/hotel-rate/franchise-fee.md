> A Franchise Fee (also called a Royalty Fee) is the recurring annual fee paid by a hotel owner or franchisee to a hotel brand (the franchisor) for the right to operate under that brand name, use its reservations system, access its loyalty programme, and follow its standards. It is typically expressed as a percentage of the property's Gross Room Revenue (GRR) and is the primary ongoing cost of operating a franchised hotel, distinct from the initial licence application fee and from the Management Fee paid to an operator.

Franchise fees are disclosed in the Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) that hotel brands must file in the United States and in equivalent pre-sale information documents in other jurisdictions. Major US brands charge royalty fees typically in the range of 4–7 % of Gross Room Revenue (GRR): Marriott International's franchise agreements specify royalty fees of 5–6 % of GRR for core brands (Courtyard, Fairfield); Hilton's agreements charge 5–6 % for Hampton Inn and Hilton Garden Inn; and IHG charges approximately 5–6 % for Holiday Inn and Holiday Inn Express. Beyond the royalty, franchise agreements bundle additional fees — an Advertising/Marketing Fund contribution (typically 1–4 % of GRR) that funds brand-level marketing, a Programme/Technology Fee (covering the global reservation system, loyalty programme infrastructure, and revenue-management tools), a Frequent Guest Programme Fee, and sometimes a Loyalty Redemption Fee. Together these fees typically total 10–18 % of GRR for full-service brands and can materially constrain a hotel's GOP. Franchise fees flow entirely to the brand company (the franchisor) and do not overlap with the management fee, which flows to the operator. In asset-light hotel structures where ownership, franchising, and management are fully separated, a single hotel may pay a franchise fee to a brand company while also paying a management fee to an independent operator or third-party management company.

# Distinctions
- **Franchise Fee** vs [Management Fee](/lodging/hotel-rate/management-fee.md) — A Franchise Fee is paid by the hotel owner to the brand company (franchisor) for the right to operate under the brand and access its systems; a Management Fee is paid by the hotel owner to the hotel management company (operator) for the day-to-day operation of the property. In a fully integrated structure one company may collect both if it is both the brand and the operator, but under the modern asset-light model these fees flow to two different entities for two distinct services.
- **Franchise Fee** vs [Rack Rate](/lodging/hotel-rate/rack-rate.md) — The Rack Rate is the undiscounted published room rate that a hotel charges guests for a stay; the Franchise Fee is a recurring percentage of room revenue paid by the hotel owner to the brand. Rack rate is a pricing concept; franchise fee is a contractual cost of brand affiliation that affects the hotel's operating economics.
