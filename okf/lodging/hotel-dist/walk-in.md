> A hotel guest who arrives at the front desk without a prior reservation and requests accommodation. Walk-ins are accommodated subject to availability, typically at the Best Available Rate (BAR) or Rack Rate, and represent a small but operationally significant portion of hotel arrivals, especially at city-center and airport properties.

Walk-in guests represent demand that was not captured during the advance booking window, creating both an opportunity and an uncertainty for the revenue manager. During high-occupancy periods a property may turn away walk-ins or upsell premium rooms at full rack rate; during low-occupancy periods, front-desk agents have latitude to offer discounts to fill inventory. In property management systems, a walk-in is typically entered as a same-day reservation with a walk-in source code. Walk-in conversion — the rate at which inquiring walk-ins are checked in — is a front-desk performance metric in full-service hotels.

**한국어 / Korean** — **워크인(Walk-In)** — 사전 예약 없이 프런트 데스크에 도착하여 숙박을 요청하는 호텔 투숙객. 워크인은 객실 가용 여부에 따라 수용되며, 일반적으로 최적가용요금(BAR) 또는 공시 요금(Rack Rate)이 적용된다.

워크인 투숙객은 사전 예약 창에서 확보되지 않은 수요로, 수익 관리자에게 기회이자 불확실성이 된다. 높은 점유율 시기에 호텔은 워크인을 거절하거나 공시 요금에 프리미엄 객실을 업셀할 수 있고, 낮은 점유율 시기에는 프런트 직원이 재량으로 할인을 제공하기도 한다.

**Aliases:** `Walk-In Guest`, `Walk-Up`, `Drop-In Guest`

# Distinctions
- **Walk-In** vs [No-Show](/common/customer/no-show.md) — A no-show had a confirmed reservation but did not arrive; a walk-in had no reservation and did arrive — they are opposite failure modes of the demand-capture process.
- **Walk-In** vs [Overbooking](/air/air-ops/overbooking.md) — Overbooking is the deliberate practice of accepting more reservations than available rooms to hedge against no-shows; walk-ins are unplanned arrivals who compete with reservation holders for those rooms.
