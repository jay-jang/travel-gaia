> A scheduled air service that operates between an origin and a destination airport with no intermediate landings. It is the fastest travel option on a given city pair and is distinguished from a through flight (same flight number but with one or more commercial stops) and a connecting flight (which requires a change of aircraft at an intermediate airport).

In IATA scheduling terminology a nonstop service has a single flight number, no intermediate points, and the aircraft does not land between the origin and destination. This is distinct from a "through" (or "direct") service, which may carry the same flight number through a stop where passengers board or deplane. From a passenger perspective nonstop equates to no change of aircraft and no stop; it is the premium positioning used by airlines on competitive long-haul routes. In IATA SSIM schedules, nonstop operations are indicated by the absence of any waypoint or via city in the leg structure. Revenue management and scheduling systems track city-pair demand separately for nonstop vs connecting routings, since nonstop commands a premium in willingness-to-pay.

**한국어 / Korean** — **무착륙 직항편(Nonstop Flight)** — 출발 공항에서 목적지 공항까지 중간 착륙 없이 운항하는 정기 항공 서비스. 같은 편명으로 하나 이상의 상업적 기착이 있는 직항 편(through flight)이나 중간 공항에서 항공기를 갈아타야 하는 환승 편(connecting flight)과 구별되는 가장 빠른 이동 옵션이다.

IATA 일정 관련 용어에서 무착륙 서비스는 단일 편명을 사용하고, 중간 기착지가 없으며, 출발지와 목적지 사이에서 항공기가 착륙하지 않는다. 이는 승객이 탑승하거나 하기하는 기착지가 있더라도 같은 편명으로 운항할 수 있는 직항 편(through/direct)과 구별된다. 승객 입장에서 무착륙은 항공기 교체 없음 및 기착 없음을 의미하며, 경쟁적인 장거리 노선에서 항공사가 프리미엄으로 내세우는 요소다.

# Distinctions
- **Nonstop Flight** vs Through Flight — A nonstop flight has zero intermediate landings; a through flight operates under the same flight number but makes one or more commercial stops at intermediate airports where passengers may board or deplane.
- **Nonstop Flight** vs Stopover — A stopover is a passenger-initiated break in journey at an intermediate city; a nonstop flight simply has no intermediate city at all.
- **Nonstop Flight** vs Technical Stop — A technical stop is an intermediate landing for operational purposes (fuel, crew) with no commercial boarding; a nonstop flight has no intermediate landing whatsoever.
