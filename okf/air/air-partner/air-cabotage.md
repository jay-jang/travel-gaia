> Air cabotage is the carriage of passengers, mail, or cargo for remuneration between two points within the territory of a single state by an aircraft registered in, or operated by, a carrier of another state. Article 7 of the 1944 Chicago Convention entitles each contracting state to refuse permission to foreign carriers to operate such domestic traffic, making air cabotage a sovereign right rather than a default freedom.

Air cabotage corresponds to the Eighth Freedom of the Air (true cabotage: carry domestic traffic within a foreign state as an extension of, or separate from, an international service) and partially to the Seventh Freedom. Chicago Convention Article 7 expressly forbids contracting states from granting cabotage rights on an exclusive basis to any other single state. In practice, cabotage remains prohibited in most bilateral Air Services Agreements: US carriers cannot operate domestic routes in Europe, and vice versa, outside the EU single aviation area. The European Union is the most significant exception: EU liberalisation completed in 1997 created a single aviation market in which any EU-certificated carrier may operate cabotage routes within any other EU member state. Air cabotage should not be confused with maritime cabotage (governed by separate maritime law frameworks) nor with the Freedoms of the Air, which define traffic rights between states rather than within one state.

# Distinctions
- **Air Cabotage** vs [Cabotage (Maritime)](/cruise/cruise/cabotage.md) — Air cabotage is governed by the Chicago Convention and bilateral ASAs; maritime cabotage is governed by separate maritime law (e.g., Jones Act in the USA, EU maritime regulation).
- **Air Cabotage** vs [Freedoms of the Air](/air/air-partner/freedoms-of-the-air.md) — The Freedoms of the Air define rights to carry traffic between states (1st–7th freedom) or within a foreign state (8th freedom = true cabotage); air cabotage specifically equates to the 8th freedom.
- **Air Cabotage** vs [Open Skies Agreement](/air/air-partner/open-skies-agreement.md) — Open skies agreements liberalise international air services (typically 1st–5th freedoms) but do not normally include cabotage rights; the EU single aviation area is exceptional in including cabotage within the bloc.
