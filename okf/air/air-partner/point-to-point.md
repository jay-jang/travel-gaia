> An airline network model in which routes connect origin and destination cities directly without routing passengers through intermediate hub airports. Dominant among low-cost carriers, the model eliminates connection time and hub-dependency costs, achieves higher aircraft utilization through fast gate turns, but requires sufficient origin-to-destination traffic to sustain each route independently.

In a point-to-point network, every route must justify itself on O&D (Origin & Destination) demand alone; passengers do not rely on interline connections. This model was pioneered by Southwest Airlines in the United States and refined by European LCCs (Ryanair, easyJet). Key operational advantages: (1) Higher aircraft utilization — short sector lengths with fast turnarounds (25-30 minutes) allow more daily rotations per aircraft; (2) Simpler operations — no transfer baggage handling or minimum connection time management; (3) Secondary airport strategy — many LCCs use lower-cost secondary airports to reduce slot and ground-handling costs. Trade-offs: point-to-point airlines typically do not participate in codeshare or interline agreements, and thin routes to small cities require a minimum demand threshold that legacy hub-and-spoke networks serve better by funnelling connecting traffic. The distinction is not binary — some hybrid carriers use a mix of hub-and-spoke for long-haul and point-to-point for short-haul.

**한국어 / Korean** — **포인트 투 포인트(Point-to-Point) / 직항 노선 모델** — 승객을 중간 허브 공항을 통하지 않고 출발지와 목적지를 직접 연결하는 항공사 네트워크 모델. 저가 항공사 사이에서 지배적이며, 환승 시간과 허브 의존 비용을 제거하고 빠른 게이트 회전을 통해 더 높은 항공기 가동률을 달성한다.

# Distinctions
- **Point-to-Point** vs [Hub-and-Spoke](/air/air-partner/hub-and-spoke.md) — Hub-and-Spoke funnels passengers from many origins through central hub airports for onward connection, allowing legacy carriers to serve thin O&D markets by aggregating traffic. Point-to-Point bypasses hubs entirely; each route needs independent O&D demand.
- **Point-to-Point** vs [Nonstop Flight](/air/air-ops/nonstop-flight.md) — A Nonstop Flight is an operational descriptor for a single flight that does not stop en route; Point-to-Point is a network architecture. A hub-and-spoke carrier can operate nonstop flights between two cities as part of its hub feeding strategy; the difference lies in how the whole network is structured, not in whether individual flights have stops.
