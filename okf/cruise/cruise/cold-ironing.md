> The practice of supplying shore-side electrical power to a docked ship so that its main and auxiliary engines can be shut down while in port, eliminating exhaust emissions from onboard generators. Also termed Alternative Maritime Power (AMP) or Shore-Side Electricity (SSE), it is a key operational measure for reducing cruise-ship and cargo-vessel emissions at berth and is mandated at major ports including those in California and several European cities.

When a ship is at berth it must power its hotel load (lighting, ventilation, refrigeration, passenger amenities) and auxiliary machinery. Traditionally this is done by running onboard diesel generators, which emit NOx, SOx, particulate matter, and CO2 directly into the port environment. Cold ironing connects the vessel to the local electricity grid via a high-voltage shore power cable, shutting down the generators. Benefits include: elimination of port-area air pollution from the ship, reduced noise and vibration for port neighborhoods, and — where grid power is from renewable sources — significant CO2 reductions. Challenges include the capital cost of shore-side infrastructure and onboard converter systems (ships operate at 60 Hz in the Americas, 50 Hz in Europe), voltage and frequency conversion, and the need for standardized connectors (IEC/ISO 80005 series). The California Air Resources Board (CARB) At-Berth Regulation (2007) was the first mandatory regime requiring cruise ships calling at California ports to use shore power or an equivalent emission-control technology. IMO's 2023 GHG Strategy explicitly lists shore power as a mid-term measure.

**한국어 / Korean** — **콜드 아이어닝(Cold Ironing) / 육상 전력 공급(Shore Power)** — 정박 중인 선박에 육상 전력을 공급하여 주기관 및 보조 엔진을 정지시킴으로써 항내 발전기로 인한 배기 배출을 제거하는 관행. 대체 해양 전력(AMP) 또는 육상 전력 공급(SSE)이라고도 하며, 크루즈선과 화물선의 정박 중 배출량을 줄이는 핵심 운영 조치이다.

# Distinctions
- **Cold Ironing** vs [Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII)](/cruise/cruise/carbon-intensity-indicator-cii.md) — CII is an IMO metric that rates a ship's annual carbon intensity while at sea; Cold Ironing reduces emissions specifically while the vessel is at berth and engines would otherwise idle, complementing at-sea efficiency measures but measured and regulated differently.
