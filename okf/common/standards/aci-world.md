> The international trade body representing airports globally, with membership covering over 700 operators and more than 1,900 airports in over 180 countries and territories. ACI World advocates for airports in relations with international organisations and governments, sets airport industry standards, and co-publishes the Worldwide Airport Slot Guidelines (WASG) jointly with IATA and the WWACG.

ACI World (headquartered in Geneva) works through five regional offices to represent airports in the ICAO system and at intergovernmental bodies. Key programmes include the Airport Service Quality (ASQ) Programme (the world's largest passenger satisfaction benchmark, covering over 390 airports), Airport Carbon Accreditation (ACA), the ACI Airport Health Accreditation (AHA), and APEX in Safety. ACI World co-publishes the Worldwide Airport Slot Guidelines (WASG) with IATA and WWACG, participates in joint governance of the Worldwide Airport Slot Board (WASB), and co-manages CUTE standards. ACI World also publishes the World Airport Traffic Forecasts (WATF) and is the principal counterpart to IATA in bilateral work on airport charges, service standards, and facilitation policies.

**한국어 / Korean** — **국제공항협의회(ACI World)** — ACI World는 전 세계 공항을 대표하는 국제 무역 단체로, 180개 이상의 국가·지역에서 700개 이상의 운영자와 1,900개 이상의 공항을 회원으로 보유한다. 국제기구 및 정부와의 관계에서 공항을 대변하며, IATA·WWACG와 공동으로 WASG 등 핵심 글로벌 지침을 발행하고 공항 서비스 품질(ASQ)·탄소 인증(ACA) 등 주요 프로그램을 운영한다.

# Distinctions
- **ACI World (Airports Council International)** vs [CUTE](/common/standards/cute.md) — CUTE is the technical standard allowing multiple airlines to share airport infrastructure; ACI World is the trade body and governance partner overseeing CUTE standards — the organisation versus the programme.
- **ACI World (Airports Council International)** vs [Worldwide Airport Slot Guidelines (WASG)](/air/air-partner/wasg.md) — The WASG is the published slot-allocation framework; ACI World is one of the three co-publishers (with IATA and WWACG) and a co-governance member of the WASB that oversees it — the organisation versus the document it helps create.
