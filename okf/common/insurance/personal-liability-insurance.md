> A travel insurance component or standalone product that covers the policyholder's legal liability to pay compensation to a third party — a person or property owner — for accidental bodily injury or accidental property damage caused by the traveler during the insured trip. Distinct from Personal Accident Insurance (PAI), which covers the insured person's own injury or death rather than their liability to others.

Personal liability cover typically pays legal defence costs and compensation up to a policy limit (commonly £1–5 million or equivalent) if the traveler is held legally responsible for injuring another person or damaging their property. Common covered scenarios include accidentally breaking hotel room property, injuring another skier on the slopes, or causing a pedestrian accident while cycling. Most policies exclude motorised vehicle liability, professional activities, wilful acts, and contractual liability. In the UK, personal liability is a standard element of comprehensive travel insurance policies and a separate regulated product category. It is distinct from Personal Accident Insurance (PAI), which pays a benefit to the insured themselves, not to a third party.

**한국어 / Korean** — **개인 배상책임보험(Personal Liability Insurance)** — 여행 중 피보험자가 제3자에게 우발적인 신체 상해나 재물 손해를 입혔을 때 법적 배상 책임을 보장하는 여행보험 구성 요소 또는 단독 상품. 피보험자 본인의 상해·사망을 보장하는 PAI와 구별된다. 영국에서는 종합 여행보험의 표준 보장 요소다.

# Distinctions
- **Personal Liability Insurance** vs Personal Accident Insurance (PAI) — PAI pays a fixed benefit directly to the insured traveler for their own accidental death or injury; Personal Liability Insurance pays compensation to a third party harmed by the insured traveler's actions.
- **Personal Liability Insurance** vs Comprehensive Travel Insurance — Comprehensive travel insurance is a bundled policy combining multiple coverages; personal liability insurance is one of those component coverages, and can also be purchased as a standalone product.
