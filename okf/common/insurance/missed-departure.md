> Missed departure is a travel insurance peril that pays a benefit when a traveler fails to reach the scheduled departure point — airport, ferry or cruise terminal, or rail station — in time to board the booked transport due to a covered cause such as public transport failure, the traveler's own vehicle breaking down, or severe traffic disruption. It is distinct from trip cancellation (which covers a pre-departure decision not to travel) and from missed connection (which covers failing to board a connecting service mid-journey).

Missed departure typically covers the cost of additional transport or accommodation needed to catch up with the original trip at a later point (e.g., flying to a cruise ship's next port of call or taking a later train to the holiday destination). Covered reasons vary by policy but commonly include: the public transport vehicle being cancelled or significantly delayed; the traveler's own vehicle suffering a breakdown; an accident on the route causing severe traffic delay. Not covered reasons usually include: the traveler simply setting off too late, foreseeable disruption (e.g., a known industrial strike), or disruption affecting the booked transport itself (which falls under separate delay or cancellation cover). The UK Financial Ombudsman Service handles a significant number of missed departure disputes centred on causation and evidence.

# Distinctions
- **Missed Departure** vs [Trip Cancellation](/common/insurance/trip-cancellation.md) — Trip cancellation pays when the traveler decides (or is forced) not to travel at all, before departure, for a covered reason; missed departure pays when the traveler intends to travel but fails to reach the departure point in time due to a covered cause en route.
- **Missed Departure** vs [Missed Connection](/common/insurance/missed-connection.md) — A missed connection arises mid-journey when a traveler fails to board a connecting service because a prior leg ran late; missed departure arises at the very start of the journey when the traveler cannot reach the first departure point in time.
- **Missed Departure** vs [Trip Delay](/common/insurance/trip-delay.md) — Trip delay cover usually responds to a delayed departure of the booked transport itself (the flight or train is late); missed departure responds to the traveler failing to arrive at the point of departure on time regardless of whether the transport departs on time.
