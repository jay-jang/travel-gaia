> An unplanned redirection of a flight in progress to an airport other than its scheduled destination, caused by weather, mechanical issues, medical emergencies, security threats, airport closure, or ATC direction. A diversion triggers specific carrier obligations under EU Regulation 261/2004 and equivalent passenger rights frameworks, and activates IROPS recovery procedures including re-accommodation and duty-of-care provision.

A diversion occurs when the flight crew, airline operations control, or air traffic control determines that landing at the scheduled destination is not safe or possible and redirects the aircraft to an alternate airport. Common causes include: destination weather below landing minimums; en-route severe weather; medical emergency requiring immediate landing; aircraft technical defect; security incident; runway closure at destination. Under EU Regulation 261/2004 a diversion to an airport other than the one booked generally constitutes a significant delay and/or a change of destination, potentially triggering compensation and the full suite of care obligations. The operating carrier must arrange onward transport to the scheduled destination or return passengers to the departure airport.

**한국어 / Korean** — **항공편 회항(Flight Diversion)** — 기상, 기계적 결함, 기내 의료 응급상황, 보안 위협, 공항 폐쇄 또는 항공 교통 관제 지시 등으로 인해 운항 중인 항공편이 계획된 목적지 이외의 공항으로 비계획적으로 방향을 바꾸는 것. 회항은 EU 규정 261/2004 및 이에 상응하는 승객 권리 체계에 따른 운송인의 특정 의무를 발동시키며, 재예약 및 돌봄 의무를 포함한 IROPS 복구 절차를 가동시킨다.

# Distinctions
- **Flight Diversion** vs [Technical Stop](/air/air-ops/technical-stop.md) — A technical stop is a pre-planned operational landing at an intermediate point with no passenger boarding or deplaning; a flight diversion is an unplanned, emergency-driven landing at an airport other than the filed destination, typically with significant disruption to the passenger journey.
- **Flight Diversion** vs [IROPS](/air/air-partner/irops.md) — IROPS (Irregular Operations) is the broad category of any unplanned event that disrupts normal airline operations, of which a flight diversion is one specific type; other IROPS include delays, cancellations, and equipment swaps.
- **Flight Diversion** vs [Force Majeure](/common/disruption/force-majeure.md) — Force majeure is a legal doctrine exempting a carrier from obligations when an event beyond its control makes performance impossible; a flight diversion may be caused by force-majeure events (extreme weather) but is often caused by carrier-controllable factors (mechanical defect), and the passenger rights consequences differ depending on whether the cause qualifies as extraordinary circumstances.
