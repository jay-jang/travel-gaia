> Airline insolvency is the condition in which an airline is unable to meet its financial obligations, typically resulting in its suspension of operations, appointment of an administrator or liquidator, and the stranding of passengers who hold tickets for cancelled flights. It is distinct from ordinary cancellation or disruption events because the insolvent carrier cannot fulfil its duty of care or rerouting obligations, leaving passenger protection dependent on travel insurance, payment-card chargebacks, ATOL/ABTA protection, or IATA member airline rescue fares.

Airlines operate on thin margins and are exposed to fuel price volatility, demand shocks, and competitive pressures that periodically result in high-profile failures — including Monarch Airlines (UK, 2017), WOW Air (Iceland, 2019), Flybe (UK, 2020, re-entered administration 2023), and Thomas Cook Airlines (2019). In the EU, Regulation 261/2004 requires the operating carrier to provide care and assistance, but insolvency proceedings may prevent the carrier from funding those obligations. In the UK, the ATOL scheme guarantees package-holiday travellers repatriation or refund when the licence holder fails — extended ad hoc to cover all Monarch passengers in 2017. IATA member airlines may offer rescue fares to stranded passengers under IATA's Airlines Customer Commitment. From the distribution perspective, BSP and ARC have specific procedures for handling insolvency events, and Supplier Default Coverage in travel insurance is the primary consumer financial protection for standalone air tickets outside ATOL.

Monarch Airlines(영국, 2017), WOW Air(아이슬란드, 2019), Flybe(영국, 2020·2023), Thomas Cook Airlines(2019) 등 고프로파일 항공사 실패 사례가 발생했다. 영국의 ATOL 제도는 패키지 여행자를 보호하며, 2017년 모나크 붕괴 시 정부가 ATOL 보호 여부와 무관하게 모든 영향받은 승객을 송환하는 데 임시 활용하였다.

# Distinctions
- **Airline Insolvency** vs [Force Majeure](/common/disruption/force-majeure.md) — Force majeure covers extraordinary events outside the carrier's control that excuse obligations; airline insolvency is not force majeure — it is caused by financial mismanagement or market failure and does not relieve passengers of their contractual entitlements under consumer protection law, even if the carrier cannot practically fulfil them.
- **Airline Insolvency** vs [Air Travel Organiser's Licence](/common/insurance/atol.md) — ATOL is a UK consumer protection scheme that bonds tour operators and flight sellers, guaranteeing package-holiday customers can be repatriated or refunded if the licence holder fails; airline insolvency is the event that triggers ATOL claims, but ATOL coverage does not extend to standalone airline tickets not sold as part of a licensable package.
- **Airline Insolvency** vs [Supplier Default Coverage](/common/insurance/supplier-default-coverage.md) — Supplier Default Coverage is a travel insurance benefit reimbursing pre-paid costs when a travel supplier becomes insolvent; airline insolvency is the underlying risk event. The coverage is the financial protection instrument; insolvency is the trigger condition it responds to.
