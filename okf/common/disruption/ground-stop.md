> A Ground Stop (GS) is an Air Traffic Flow Management (ATFM) initiative that requires aircraft meeting specified criteria to remain on the ground at their departure airport, preventing them from departing until the stop is lifted. It is the most restrictive Traffic Management Initiative (TMI) available to air traffic control authorities, used when a destination airport, airspace sector, or en-route segment cannot safely absorb additional traffic.

A Ground Stop may be airport-specific (all flights to a single destination held), geography-specific (flights into a region held), or equipment-specific (flights that rely on particular navigation or communication systems held). In the United States the FAA Air Traffic Control System Command Center (ATCSCC) coordinates ground stops; individual ATC facilities may impose a local GS of up to 15 minutes without ATCSCC notification, or up to 30 minutes with notification, before the ATCSCC takes coordination responsibility. Ground stops are generally short-term, lasting two hours or less, and take priority over all other TMIs including Expect Departure Clearance Times (EDCT) from Ground Delay Programs. Common triggers include severe weather at the destination, runway or taxiway work, equipment outages, security incidents, or VIP movements. A GS directly causes IRROPS for affected passengers: flights are cancelled or severely delayed, triggering carrier duty-of-care obligations under applicable passenger-rights regulations.

**한국어 / Korean** — **그라운드 스톱(지상 대기 명령)** — 그라운드 스톱(GS)은 특정 기준을 충족하는 항공기를 출발 공항에서 지상 대기시키는 항공 교통 흐름 관리(ATFM) 조치로, 해제 시까지 출발을 금지한다. 목적지 공항·공역 구간·항로가 추가 교통량을 안전하게 수용할 수 없을 때 항공 교통 관제 당국이 사용하는 가장 제한적인 교통 관리 이니셔티브(TMI)다.

그라운드 스톱은 특정 목적지·특정 지역·특정 장비별로 부과될 수 있다. 미국에서는 FAA ATCSCC가 조율하며 개별 ATC 시설은 최대 30분까지 로컬 GS를 부과할 수 있다. 통상 2시간 이하의 단기 조치로 GDP의 EDCT보다 우선하며, 악천후·활주로 공사·장비 장애·보안 사고 등이 주요 원인이다.

# Distinctions
- **Ground Stop** vs [Irregular Operations (IRROPS)](/common/disruption/irrops.md) — A Ground Stop is a specific ATFM tool issued by ATC to hold aircraft on the ground; IRROPS is the broader term for any material flight timetable deviation, of which a ground stop is one common cause.
- **Ground Stop** vs [Flight Diversion](/common/disruption/flight-diversion.md) — A Ground Stop prevents aircraft from departing by holding them at the origin; a flight diversion reroutes an airborne aircraft to an alternate airport.
- **Ground Stop** vs [Force Majeure](/common/disruption/force-majeure.md) — Force majeure is a legal concept excusing contractual obligations; a ground stop is an ATC operational measure that may be triggered by force-majeure conditions but is itself a regulatory tool, not a legal doctrine.
