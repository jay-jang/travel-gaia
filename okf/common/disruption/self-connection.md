> An itinerary composed of two or more separately ticketed flights that a traveller combines independently, without an interline agreement between the carriers. Because no single through-ticket exists, carriers bear no contractual obligation to rebook or compensate the traveller if the connection is missed.

In a traditional interline booking a single through-ticket links all segments and the carrying carriers share joint liability; a self-connection breaks that chain — each ticket is a standalone contract. When the first flight is delayed and the passenger misses the second, the second carrier treats the no-show as a voluntary cancellation and is not obliged to rebook or refund. OTAs such as Kiwi.com pioneered 'virtual interlining' — algorithmically pairing compatible self-connections and offering a proprietary missed-connection guarantee (self-transfer protection) as a paid add-on to bridge the liability gap. IATA has noted in its Interline guidance that self-connection itineraries lack the safety-net passengers expect from formally interlining carriers.

**한국어 / Korean** — **셀프 커넥션(Self-Connection)** — 항공사 간 인터라인 협정 없이 여행자가 개별적으로 결합한 별도 발권 항공권 두 편 이상으로 구성된 여정. 단일 통합 항공권이 없으므로, 첫 항공편 지연으로 연결편을 놓쳐도 항공사는 재예약·배상 의무를 지지 않는다.

전통적 인터라인 예약에서는 단일 통합 항공권이 모든 구간을 연결하고 운송 항공사들이 공동 책임을 진다. 셀프 커넥션은 이 사슬을 끊어, 각 항공권이 독립적 계약이 된다. Kiwi.com 등 OTA는 '버추얼 인터라이닝'을 개척해 호환 셀프 커넥션을 알고리즘으로 짝짓고, 유료 부가 상품(self-transfer protection)을 제공한다.

**Aliases:** `Virtual Interlining`, `Self-Transfer Connection`, `Non-Interlined Connection`

# Distinctions
- **Self-Connection** vs [Misconnect](/common/disruption/misconnect.md) — A misconnect is a missed connection on a through-ticket or formally interlined booking, triggering the carrier's obligation to rebook and potentially compensate; a self-connection miss on separate tickets carries no such obligation — the second carrier is entitled to treat it as a no-show.
- **Self-Connection** vs [Interline](/air/air-partner/interline.md) — Interline is a commercial and technical agreement allowing carriers to sell seats on each other's flights under a single ticket with joint responsibility; a self-connection has no such agreement — each carrier is liable only for its own flight under its own ticket.
