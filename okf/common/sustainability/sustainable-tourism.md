> Sustainable tourism is tourism that takes full account of its current and future economic, social, and environmental impacts, addressing the needs of visitors, the industry, the environment, and host communities. As defined by the United Nations World Tourism Organization (UNWTO), it applies the principles of sustainability to all segments of the tourism sector and requires a continuous monitoring of impacts, introducing preventive and corrective measures wherever necessary.

The UNWTO defines three pillars of sustainable tourism: (1) Environmental sustainability — minimizing the ecological footprint of tourism activities, protecting biodiversity, conserving natural resources, and reducing carbon emissions and waste; (2) Social and cultural sustainability — preserving cultural heritage and traditional values, respecting local communities and their ways of life, and ensuring that tourism benefits are equitably distributed to host communities; (3) Economic sustainability — generating viable, long-term employment and business opportunities for local stakeholders. In practice, sustainable tourism is operationalized through certification schemes (GSTC Criteria, Green Key, Travelife), destination management policies, and corporate sustainability strategies. UNWTO and UNEP jointly published the foundational guidance document "Making Tourism More Sustainable — A Guide for Policy Makers" (2005).

**한국어 / Korean** — **지속 가능한 관광(Sustainable Tourism)** — 지속 가능한 관광은 방문객, 산업, 환경, 지역 사회의 필요를 충족시키면서 현재와 미래의 경제적·사회적·환경적 영향을 충분히 고려하는 관광이다. UNWTO의 정의에 따르면, 지속 가능성의 원칙을 관광 부문의 모든 분야에 적용하고 영향을 지속적으로 모니터링하며 필요한 경우 예방 및 시정 조치를 도입하는 것을 요구한다.

UNWTO는 지속 가능한 관광의 세 가지 기둥으로 환경 지속 가능성, 사회적·문화적 지속 가능성, 경제적 지속 가능성을 정의한다. 실무에서는 인증 제도(GSTC Criteria, Green Key, Travelife), 목적지 관리 정책, 기업 지속 가능성 전략을 통해 구현된다.

# Distinctions
- **Sustainable Tourism** vs [Regenerative Tourism](/common/sustainability/regenerative-tourism.md) — Sustainable tourism aims to maintain the current state of natural and cultural resources so that future generations can continue to enjoy them — it is a "do no net harm" goal; regenerative tourism goes further by actively seeking to restore and improve ecosystems, communities, and cultural heritage beyond their baseline state. Regenerative tourism is thus the more ambitious successor to the sustainable tourism framework.
- **Sustainable Tourism** vs [Overtourism](/common/sustainability/overtourism.md) — Overtourism is the condition that arises when unsustainable tourism volume exceeds a destination's carrying capacity, degrading the visitor experience and harming host communities and the environment; sustainable tourism is the management approach and policy framework designed to prevent overtourism and achieve a balance between tourism growth and long-term wellbeing.
- **Sustainable Tourism** vs [GSTC Criteria](/common/sustainability/gstc-criteria.md) — The GSTC Criteria are the technical standards — a baseline of industry-agreed indicators and requirements — that operationalize sustainable tourism; sustainable tourism is the overarching concept and goal, while GSTC Criteria are one of the key tools for measuring and certifying progress toward it.
