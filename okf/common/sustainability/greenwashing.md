> Greenwashing is the practice of making false, misleading, or unsubstantiated environmental claims about a product, service, or company in order to appeal to environmentally conscious consumers or investors. In travel, it occurs when airlines, hotels, cruise lines, or tour operators overstate sustainability credentials — such as carbon neutrality, nature-friendliness, or offset coverage — without adequate evidence or verification.

Greenwashing takes many forms in travel: vague claims ("eco-friendly", "sustainable", "green") without certification, overstated carbon offset coverage that understates the actual flight footprint, misleading "carbon neutral" labels where offsets are of low quality or small scope, or cherry-picking a single initiative (e.g., in-flight recycling) to imply broader environmental responsibility. In the US, the FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260) require that environmental marketing claims be truthful, non-deceptive, and substantiated with reliable evidence; companies in breach face civil penalties. In the EU, the Green Claims Directive (proposed 2023) requires substantiation and independent verification of explicit environmental claims before they may be made. The travel sector faces particular greenwashing risk around carbon neutrality and carbon offsets: the Delta Airlines class-action (2023) and similar cases illustrate enforcement pressure on airlines that market themselves as carbon neutral based primarily on voluntary offset purchases of contested quality. Regulators expect claims to reflect the full scope of emissions, not merely selected segments.

# Distinctions
- **Greenwashing** vs [Carbon Neutral](/common/sustainability/carbon-neutral.md) — Carbon Neutral is a legitimate status achieved when all residual emissions are offset by credible, verified removals or reductions; greenwashing is the act of falsely or misleadingly claiming carbon neutrality (or similar status) without adequate evidence or using low-integrity offsets.
- **Greenwashing** vs [Carbon Offset](/common/sustainability/carbon-offset.md) — A carbon offset is a legitimate instrument that, if high-quality, can reduce the net emissions footprint of a travel purchase; greenwashing is the misrepresentation of the scope, quality, or effect of offset purchases to inflate environmental credentials.
- **Greenwashing** vs [Science-Based Targets initiative (SBTi)](/common/sustainability/science-based-targets-initiative.md) — SBTi sets independently verified emission-reduction pathways aligned with climate science; companies that commit to SBTi targets and report transparently have a verifiable basis for sustainability claims, as opposed to greenwashing which relies on unverified or insufficient evidence.
