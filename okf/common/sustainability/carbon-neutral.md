> A state in which the net carbon dioxide (CO₂) or CO₂-equivalent (CO₂e) greenhouse gas emissions attributable to a defined subject — an organisation, product, event, or building — are zero, achieved by first measuring the full carbon footprint, implementing maximum feasible emission reductions, and then counterbalancing verified residual emissions with high-quality carbon removal or avoidance credits. Carbon neutrality is formally defined by ISO 14068-1:2023 (which superseded BSI PAS 2060:2014 on 30 November 2025) and is explicitly positioned as a stepping stone toward Net Zero, not an end-state.

ISO 14068-1:2023 establishes three hierarchical requirements: (1) Measure the full carbon footprint including all material Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions; (2) Implement a documented reduction pathway — an emissions reduction plan with targets and timelines; (3) Offset only the verified residual emissions using high-quality carbon credits that meet specified criteria (real, additional, permanent, independently verified, uniquely claimed). The standard prohibits claiming carbon neutrality as a permanent outcome: claimants must set increasingly ambitious reduction targets over time and eventually transition to a net-zero state. ISO 14068-1 imposes stricter Scope 3 requirements and higher carbon-credit quality thresholds than its predecessor PAS 2060 did. In the travel and tourism sector, airlines, hotel groups, and tour operators have marketed themselves as "carbon neutral" by combining emissions reductions with offset purchases. Following widespread greenwashing scrutiny and the introduction of ISO 14068-1, many are updating their claims to specify the standard under which neutrality is assessed.

**한국어 / Korean** — **탄소 중립(Carbon Neutral)** — 조직·제품·이벤트·건물 등 정의된 대상의 탄소발자국을 측정하고, 최대한의 배출 감축을 이행한 뒤, 검증된 잔여 배출량을 고품질 탄소 제거 또는 회피 크레딧으로 상쇄하여 순 CO₂/CO₂e 배출이 제로가 된 상태. ISO 14068-1:2023(2025년 11월 30일 PAS 2060 대체)에서 공식 정의되며, 넷제로(Net Zero)를 향한 전환 단계로 위치한다.

ISO 14068-1:2023은 세 가지 계층적 요구 사항을 규정한다: (1) Scope 1·2·3 포함 전체 탄소발자국 측정; (2) 목표와 일정이 담긴 배출 감축 계획 이행; (3) 고품질 기준을 충족하는 크레딧으로만 잔여 배출 상쇄. 표준은 탄소 중립을 영구적 결과로 주장하는 것을 금지하며, 점진적으로 야심찬 감축 목표를 설정해야 한다.

# Distinctions
- **Carbon Neutral** vs [Carbon Offset](/common/sustainability/carbon-offset.md) — A Carbon Offset is the instrument — a tradeable credit representing a tonne of CO₂e reduced, removed, or avoided — used to counterbalance residual emissions in the carbon neutrality process. Carbon Neutral is the declared state that results from measuring, reducing, and then offsetting. A company can hold carbon offsets without being carbon neutral; it is carbon neutral only once offsets balance all measured emissions.
- **Carbon Neutral** vs [Fly Net Zero 2050](/common/sustainability/fly-net-zero-2050.md) — Fly Net Zero 2050 is IATA's collective commitment for global aviation to achieve net-zero carbon emissions by 2050 through SAF, new technology, operations improvements, and carbon offsets. Carbon Neutral is the ISO standard for what an individual airline, route, or flight product may claim; Fly Net Zero 2050 is the industry-wide long-term target.
- **Carbon Neutral** vs [Science-Based Targets initiative (SBTi)](/common/sustainability/science-based-targets-initiative.md) — The SBTi defines net-zero as requiring near-complete absolute emission reductions (typically 90–95%) with only a small residual addressed by permanent removal credits — carbon neutrality via heavy offset use is explicitly not SBTi net-zero. Carbon Neutral (ISO 14068-1) allows residual emissions to be addressed by both removal and avoidance credits, making it a less stringent threshold than SBTi-defined net zero.
