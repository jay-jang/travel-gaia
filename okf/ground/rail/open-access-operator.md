> An open access operator (OAO) is a railway undertaking that runs commercial passenger services on rail infrastructure it does not own, holding a license and safety certificate to operate on tracks opened to competition under European or national rail liberalization legislation. Open access operators compete with or complement the historic incumbent operator on the same routes, paying track access charges to the Infrastructure Manager.

Open access rail was established in the EU through Directive 91/440/EEC and extended to domestic passenger markets by the Fourth Railway Package (Directive (EU) 2016/2370), requiring member states to grant open access rights by December 2020. Operators must obtain: (1) a railway undertaking license from the national regulatory authority; (2) a single safety certificate under ERA's framework; and (3) capacity allocation (train paths/slots) from the Infrastructure Manager. Examples include Italo (NTV) in Italy competing with Trenitalia on high-speed routes, Ouigo España competing with Renfe, and in the UK (which pre-dates EU liberalization) Lumo and Hull Trains operating under ORR licenses on Network Rail tracks.

**한국어 / Korean** — **개방 접근 운영사(Open Access Operator / OAO)** — 소유하지 않은 철도 인프라에서 상업 여객 서비스를 운영하는 철도 사업자로, 유럽 또는 국내 철도 자유화 법령에 따라 경쟁에 개방된 선로 운영 면허 및 안전 인증을 보유한다. 동일 노선에서 기존 주요 운영사와 경쟁하거나 보완하며, 인프라 관리자에게 선로 이용 요금을 지불하고 가격·서비스·시간표로 경쟁한다.

# Distinctions
- **Open Access Operator** vs [Train Operating Company](/ground/rail/train-operating-company.md) — A Train Operating Company (TOC) holds a time-limited franchise or public service contract awarded by a government authority; an open access operator holds no franchise, competes commercially on open routes, and receives no public subsidy.
- **Open Access Operator** vs [Infrastructure Manager](/ground/rail/infrastructure-manager.md) — The infrastructure manager owns and maintains the tracks, stations, and signalling; the open access operator uses that infrastructure by paying track access charges and does not itself manage the physical rail network.
