> A business that designs, organises, and sells package travel arrangements — typically combining transport, accommodation, and other services — acting as the principal (the contracting party directly liable to the traveller), not merely as an intermediary agent.

A tour operator typically bundles flights, accommodation, ground transport, and activities into a package and sells it either directly or through travel agencies. Under the EU Package Travel Directive (2015/2302) the operator bears direct liability to the traveller for the proper performance of all package components regardless of which individual supplier fails. In the UK, air-inclusive packages require ATOL protection from the Civil Aviation Authority, and many operators also hold ABTA membership. Large operators negotiate volume-based rates — allotments, free-sale blocks, overrides — with airlines, hotels, and ground suppliers, and some vertically integrate by operating their own charter flights. The decisive characteristic is that the tour operator acts as principal: it contracts in its own name and is the traveller's counterparty, unlike a travel agency that acts as agent of the supplier or the consumer.

**한국어 / Korean** — **투어 오퍼레이터(Tour Operator)** — 교통·숙박 등 여행 구성 요소를 결합해 패키지 여행 상품을 만들고 소비자나 여행사에 판매하는 사업자. 단순 중개 대리인과 달리 계약 당사자(주체, principal)로서 여행객에 대해 직접 책임을 진다.

투어 오퍼레이터는 통상 항공편·숙박·지상 교통·액티비티를 번들로 묶어 직접 또는 여행사를 통해 패키지로 판매한다. EU 패키지 여행 지침(2015/2302) 하에서 오퍼레이터는 어느 개별 공급사가 이행에 실패하더라도 패키지 구성 요소 전체의 이행에 대해 여행자에게 직접 책임을 진다. 결정적 특징은 투어 오퍼레이터가 자신의 이름으로 계약을 맺는 주체(principal)로 행동한다는 것이며, 공급자 또는 소비자의 대리인으로 행동하는 여행사와 구별된다.

# Distinctions
- **Tour Operator** vs [Receptive Tour Operator](/tours/tour-ops/receptive-tour-operator.md) — A (outbound) tour operator designs and sells packages for travellers leaving its home market, buying ground services from a receptive operator in the destination; the receptive operator is the local specialist that handles the incoming group on the ground — the two are complementary roles on opposite ends of the supply chain.
- **Tour Operator** vs [Destination Management Company](/tours/tour-ops/destination-management-company.md) — A DMC is a local specialist in ground logistics — excursions, transfers, events — in a destination; a tour operator is the selling principal that assembles and distributes packages to end customers and may contract ground services from a DMC.
