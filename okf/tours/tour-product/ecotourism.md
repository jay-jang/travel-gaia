> Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, sustains the well-being of local people, and involves interpretation and education — as defined by The International Ecotourism Society (TIES). It is distinguished from conventional nature-based tourism by its explicit requirements for minimal environmental impact, direct financial contribution to conservation, and meaningful engagement with host communities.

The term "ecotourism" was coined in the early 1980s and formally defined by The International Ecotourism Society (TIES) in 1990. TIES identifies six principles that must be present for travel to qualify: (1) minimize physical, social, behavioral, and psychological impacts; (2) build environmental and cultural awareness and respect; (3) provide positive experiences for both visitors and hosts; (4) provide direct financial benefits for conservation; (5) generate financial benefits for both local people and private industry through community empowerment; and (6) deliver memorable interpretive experiences that raise visitors' sensitivity to host countries' environmental and political climates. The UNWTO recognizes ecotourism as a subset of sustainable tourism that has the strongest emphasis on active nature conservation. Commercially, ecotourism operators are distinguished from general adventure or nature-tourism operators by obtaining certifications from GSTC-recognized bodies (e.g., Rainforest Alliance Tourism Standard, Green Globe, national eco-certification schemes) that verify adherence to these principles. Small-group sizes, carbon-offset programs, locally owned lodges, and mandatory naturalist guide ratios are typical operational characteristics. Ecotourism is estimated to be the fastest-growing segment of the tourism industry (UNWTO), and it remains distinct from "greenwashing" — the practice of making misleading environmental claims without substantive impact — which certification schemes aim to prevent.

**한국어 / Korean** — **에코투어리즘(Ecotourism)** — 자연 지역으로의 책임 있는 여행으로, 환경을 보전하고 현지 주민의 복지를 지원하며 해설과 교육을 포함한다(TIES 정의). 환경 영향 최소화, 보전을 위한 직접적 재정 기여, 현지 지역사회와의 의미 있는 교류를 명시적으로 요구한다는 점에서 일반 자연 관광과 구분된다.

"에코투어리즘"이라는 용어는 1980년대 초에 처음 등장했으며, 1990년 국제에코투어리즘협회(TIES)에 의해 공식 정의됐다. TIES는 여섯 가지 원칙을 제시한다: (1) 물리적·사회적·행동적·심리적 영향 최소화; (2) 환경 및 문화에 대한 인식과 존중 형성; (3) 방문자와 현지 주민 모두에게 긍정적 경험 제공; (4) 보전을 위한 직접적 재정 혜택 제공; (5) 지역사회 역량 강화를 통한 재정 혜택 창출; (6) 기억에 남는 해설 경험 제공. UNWTO는 에코투어리즘을 자연 보전에 가장 강조점을 두는 지속 가능 관광의 하위 개념으로 인정한다. 에코투어리즘 운영사는 GSTC 인정 인증 기관으로부터 원칙 준수를 검증하는 인증을 취득함으로써 일반 자연 관광 운영사와 구분된다.

# Distinctions
- **Ecotourism** vs [Adventure Tourism](/tours/tour-product/adventure-tourism.md) — Adventure Tourism is defined by active physical participation in challenging activities (mountaineering, rafting, diving) and may occur in urban or developed settings; Ecotourism specifically requires travel to natural areas with explicit conservation and community-benefit obligations — overlapping when hard adventure takes place in protected ecosystems, but conceptually distinct.
- **Ecotourism** vs [Regenerative Tourism](/common/sustainability/regenerative-tourism.md) — Ecotourism aims to minimize harm and deliver net-zero or slightly positive impact through conservation contributions; Regenerative Tourism goes further, requiring a net-positive outcome — actively restoring ecosystems and social capital beyond the pre-visit state. Ecotourism is the established certification-backed standard; regenerative tourism is an emerging, more ambitious framework.
